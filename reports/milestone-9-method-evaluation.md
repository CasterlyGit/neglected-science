# Milestone 9 — method evaluation after the first investigation

Status: evaluation complete for the narrow initial thesis; broader generalization remains gated.

## Evaluation question

Can a provenance-first, adversarial selection process identify an overlooked, publicly testable robustness or reconciliation question that produces an informative result beyond a routine reading of the source paper?

## What happened

The historical Milestone 6 batch failed: direct prior art, incompatible variables, and generic theory invalidated all three finalists. The replacement manual cycle required five noncompensatory construction checks, exactly three targets, an adversarial first interview, a primary and reserve, and confirmation-grade novelty challenge. The selected E. coli question targeted a documented shared-measurement concern using a public factorial assay design with independent blocks.

The final preregistered result was not a clean positive: the published-style mean correlation was −0.644, while the independent-block estimate was −0.171 with a bootstrap 95% interval of −0.356 to 0.013. This attenuated-or-uncertain result changes what can be claimed about robustness without asserting that the published biology is false.

## Verdict on the narrow thesis

**Partially supported, not established.** The process did select a tractable robustness question whose answer was informative and adverse to an overstrong published-style interpretation. That is evidence that provenance, an explicit artifact hypothesis, public row-level data, preregistration, and a final holdout can work together.

It is not evidence that the method reliably beats ordinary expert review. The question could plausibly have been found by a careful statistical reader of the paper and Berger–Postma warning. There is no controlled comparison against ordinary review, no expert adjudication, one manually selected case, and an unresolved predeclared sensitivity. The word “reliably” therefore remains unearned.

## What to retain

- The three-target first interview and primary-plus-reserve rule. The old pilot shows why candidate ranking cannot rescue a target that fails construction.
- Evidence for and against novelty, including a named closest killer and simple alternative explanation.
- Execution-readiness certification: source accessibility and checksum verification are scientific feasibility conditions, not administrative details.
- Preregistration, independent partitions, and retaining negative/uncertain findings. They converted a tempting positive narrative into a bounded result.
- Local governed result records with source, digest, code commit, final artifact hash, disposition, and gaps.

## What to change or not generalize

- Do not claim autonomous discovery, cross-domain discovery, or superiority to ordinary review from one manually curated case.
- Do not promote automatic candidate generation or total-score ranking. Neither selected this investigation and neither has been evaluated against expert or manual alternatives.
- Require exact executable uncertainty and robustness procedures before validation freeze. The v2 amendment exposed an avoidable preregistration ambiguity.
- Require a durable-result receipt immediately after a final run. The first final result initially existed only in an ignored artifact.
- Treat a missing specialist review or unimplemented preregistered sensitivity as a live verification gap, not a documentation footnote.

## Misses, false leads, and dependencies

The historical finalists were false leads under the redesigned entry standard. Four replacement-cycle leads were deferred or excluded for inaccessible data, an already tested interaction, prior work, or unverified large archives. The selected E. coli target itself revealed an operational miss: official unattended retrieval failed despite a prior row-level inspection, requiring owner-mediated official download. It also retains two scientific dependencies: assay-block exchangeability and the unimplemented weighted errors-in-variables sensitivity.

## Justified next architecture

The justified system is a **small, provenance-first question-selection and robustness-test workflow**, not a broad opportunity-ranking platform. Its next investigation must begin only after the revised execution-readiness certificate passes for both primary and reserve. The next architecture should automate deterministic evidence and integrity checks, but leave target selection, novelty disposition, preregistration amendments, and claim review auditable and human-controlled.
