# Vision

## Purpose

Build a disciplined computational instrument that identifies neglected, important, and falsifiable scientific questions from public knowledge and data, then proves its usefulness by completing real scientific investigations.

## Initial thesis and boundary

The initial, testable thesis is narrower: **can a provenance-first, adversarial selection process reliably identify overlooked, publicly testable robustness or reconciliation questions?** Neglected Science is not yet an autonomous cross-domain discovery system. The nine milestones are the proof path for this thesis: Milestones 1–6 establish and adversarially test candidate selection, 6.5 verifies execution readiness, 7 freezes one question, 8 executes it reproducibly, and 9 evaluates whether the method surfaced a question better than ordinary review. Broader claims about cross-domain unknowns, autonomous discovery, or generalization remain gated on one or more completed investigations.

## Long-term outcome

Neglected Science should make it possible for a technically capable outsider, assisted by AI, to:

1. reconstruct the evidence and assumptions in a bounded scientific territory;
2. detect explicit gaps, contradictions, translation gaps, and underused-data opportunities;
3. produce candidate questions with evidence both for and against novelty;
4. rank candidates by importance, tractability, verification strength, and impact;
5. execute a preregistered, reproducible computational investigation;
6. expose the result and the discovery method to adversarial review; and
7. generalize only the parts that survived contact with real science.

## Non-goals

- Generating plausible papers or novelty claims quickly.
- Treating citations, model confidence, or repeated “future work” statements as proof of importance.
- Building a universal autonomous scientist before completing one investigation.
- Using private, restricted, or inaccessible data as if it were public.
- Replacing domain experts where physical infrastructure or specialist judgment is necessary.
- Optimizing for a positive finding; useful falsification and negative results count.

## Governing principles

- Evidence before narrative.
- Falsification before promotion.
- Competing hypotheses before model fitting.
- Holdouts before final claims.
- Provenance before scale.
- One verified investigation before generalization.

## Guiding program

The nine milestones in `ROADMAP.md` are the durable program. Their order may be refined when evidence requires it, but none may be silently removed, collapsed into an app-building exercise, or claimed complete without its stated gate.

After each real investigation, the program enters a local feedback cycle: preserve the result, evaluate the narrow thesis and process integrity, propose one bounded improvement, obtain owner approval for its direction and level, and test it on another real investigation. This loop is the path to robust value; it is not a license for autonomous scope expansion.
