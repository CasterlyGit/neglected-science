# Gap taxonomy

## Explicit gaps

Direct statements of unknowns, limitations, missing measurements, or proposed future work. These are inexpensive leads but often crowded, stale, or rhetorically exaggerated.

Required challenge: determine whether the gap remains open, important, and testable.

## Contradiction gaps

Findings that appear incompatible after accounting for population, setting, measurement, method, scale, and time.

Required challenge: distinguish a real contradiction from different boundary conditions or definitions.

## Translation gaps

Structurally similar concepts studied under different terminology or disciplinary assumptions.

Required challenge: establish which mathematical or causal structure is actually shared; lexical similarity is insufficient.

## Data-use gaps

Public data repeatedly used for conventional tasks while alternative falsifiable questions remain underexplored.

Required challenge: show that the proposed use is scientifically meaningful, permitted, adequately measured, and not already exhausted under another framing.

## Candidate provenance

Every candidate must identify its gap type, generating records, contrary evidence, transformation steps, and human judgments. Hybrid candidates may use multiple types but may not omit provenance.
