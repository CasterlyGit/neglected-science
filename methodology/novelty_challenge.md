# Adversarial novelty challenge

The goal is to disprove that a finalist is both novel and worth investigating.

## Required attacks

1. Search exact and alternative terminology.
2. Trace backward citations to originating claims and forward citations to later resolution.
3. Search adjacent disciplines for equivalent mechanisms or measurements.
4. Test whether differing definitions or boundary conditions explain the apparent gap.
5. Search reviews, corrections, retractions, negative findings, and failed replications.
6. Check whether the proposed public data measures the required variables adequately.
7. Construct simpler explanations and identify decisive tests between them.
8. Ask what a domain specialist would likely regard as already known or trivial.
9. Document missing access or expertise that prevents a confident novelty assessment.

## Possible dispositions

- Rejected: prior work or a fatal limitation resolves the opportunity.
- Reframed: a narrower or different question survives.
- Gated: potentially valuable but requires unavailable evidence or expertise.
- Finalist: survives current attacks with uncertainty explicitly stated.

No disposition may claim universal proof of novelty.
