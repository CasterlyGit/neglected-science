# Two-system architecture

## System 1 — evidence and question construction

System 1 builds small, source-first corpora and turns only their auditable tensions into candidate questions. Its outputs are: retrieval and exclusion receipts; source-linked claims; structural records; explicit alternative explanations; and exactly three interview-ready targets only when every construction gate passes.

System 1 is successful when a corpus changes a decision: it admits a data-shaped question, rejects a plausible but defective lead, identifies a missing evidence requirement, or documents that no justified trio exists. Record count, topical breadth, and apparent novelty do not count as success.

## System 2 — structural comparison and investigation verdict

System 2 compares only System-1 records whose system, unit, measurement, inference, validation gap, and provenance are explicit. It may identify a research-design relationship, but never a shared scientific mechanism from resemblance alone. A cross-domain comparison can seed a domain-specific lead; it cannot select an experiment or make a scientific claim.

System 2 reaches its final verdict only through a selected, preregistered, reproducible investigation, or through an evidence-backed redesign-or-stop decision. It must retain the primary, reserve, failed trios, adverse prior art, acquisition failures, nulls, and uncertainty.

## Handoff contract

1. System 1 supplies a bounded corpus and an explicit decision record.
2. System 2 maps only evidence-backed structural relations and returns a non-promotion verdict.
3. A candidate may re-enter System 1 only with a named domain-specific consequence and all construction checks.
4. A final investigation starts only after a System-1 trio yields a primary and reserve and both pass execution readiness.
